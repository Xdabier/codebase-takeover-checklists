# Ten checks before you hire a remote developer

Ten checks a founder can run on any remote or freelance developer before paying anything. None of them needs you to read code.

The full guide, with sources: **[badii.studio/how-to-vet-a-remote-developer](https://badii.studio/how-to-vet-a-remote-developer)**

## Is this a real person or business?

- [ ] **A live video call before any money.** Camera on, plain background, and the same person on every later call.
- [ ] **One name everywhere.** Same name, photo and history on LinkedIn, GitHub, their site and the contract.
- [ ] **A legal identity you can look up.** A company in a public register, a valid VAT number, or a full legal name and address.
- [ ] **Payment goes where the contract says.** Invoice and bank account in the contract's name. No third party, no crypto.

## Can they do the work?

- [ ] **Work you can trace to them.** Public code with years of history, a live product, or old work explained on a call.
- [ ] **A reference you reach yourself.** A past client, contacted through details you found, not ones you were handed.
- [ ] **A small paid first job.** Days, not months, with a written list of what you get.

## Do you keep control?

- [ ] **A contract that gives you the code.** A signed clause that assigns copyright in the work to you.
- [ ] **Your repo, your accounts.** Code, hosting and domain in your company's name. The developer is invited in.
- [ ] **The person you met does the work.** Named in the contract. Subcontracting needs your consent.

No single check decides. An honest developer can miss one for a normal reason, so ask before you judge. Two or more misses is the point to stop and ask harder questions. This is a checklist, not legal advice. The full guide gives the public sources behind each check.
